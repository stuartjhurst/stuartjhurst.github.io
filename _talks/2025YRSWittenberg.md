The Seminar for Young Researchers on (European) Labour and Social Security Law provides an international academic forum for PhD researchers working in the fields of international labour law, European employment and social security law, comparative labour law, and related national legal frameworks. Its aim is to foster scholarly exchange, critical discussion and academic collaboration among early-career researchers from different jurisdictions. Through research presentations, peer feedback and plenary discussions, the seminar offers participants an opportunity to present and develop their research, engage with comparative perspectives, and build connections within the international academic community.

I participated as a co-rapporteur for Tomasz Mirosławski's (University of Miskolc, Hungary) presentation entitled ‘Workplace Surveillance in the Visegrád Countries’. My contribution involved critically engaging with the research, offering constructive comments and questions, and contributing to the subsequent plenary discussion. The presentation provided an opportunity to reflect on the evolving relationship between technological developments, employee privacy and employers' monitoring powers, particularly from a comparative labour law perspective.
