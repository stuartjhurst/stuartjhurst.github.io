An interdisciplinary workshop organised by the Centre for Law at Work at the University of Bristol Law School, bringing together postgraduate and early career researchers to discuss contemporary issues relating to work and labour. The workshop provided a forum for researchers to present ongoing work and exchange ideas through presentations and discussion. Topics included the changing nature of work, inequalities and equality, diversity and inclusion (EDI) in the workplace, the impact of global crises on workers and labour, critical approaches to labour regulation, and relationships between workers, trade unions and employers. The workshop welcomed contributions from a range of disciplines, including employment and labour law, industrial relations, human resource management, political economy and policy studies.

I contributed to the organisation of the workshop, helping to develop and facilitate an interdisciplinary forum for postgraduate and early career researchers working on issues relating to work and labour.

I also presented aspects of my ongoing doctoral research, focusing on the relationship between psychosocial risk and disability in the workplace. The presentation generated discussion around the specific theoretical framework I am engaging (Critical Disability Theory) and provided an opportunity to receive feedback and engage with researchers working on related questions.
