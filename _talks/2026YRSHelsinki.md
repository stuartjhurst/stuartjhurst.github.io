The Seminar for Young Researchers on (European) Labour and Social Security Law brings together doctoral researchers working in international labour law, European employment and social security law, comparative labour law, and related national legal frameworks. It seeks to encourage international academic collaboration and the exchange of ideas between early-career researchers through the presentation and critical discussion of ongoing research projects. Combining individual research presentations, peer feedback and collective academic discussion, the seminar provides a forum for exploring contemporary developments and challenges in labour and social security law across different legal systems, while fostering connections within the international research community.

I presented some preliminary findings from my doctoral research. The presentation introduced the theoretical and conceptual foundations of my project before examining corporeal ableism and the ways in which psychosocial hazards are understood and addressed in the workplace. In particular, I explored the relationship between legal constructions of disability, non-evident and invisible disabilities, epistemic injustice, and the provision of reasonable adjustments. The presentation provided an opportunity to engage with fellow early-career researchers and established academics on these issues, situating my research within broader debates on disability, equality and the regulation of working life.
