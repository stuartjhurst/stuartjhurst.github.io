The Early Career Seminars on Comparative Labour Law (ECOLL), organised by the Labour Law Community (LLC), bring together early-career labour law scholars from different legal and academic backgrounds to explore contemporary issues in labour law through a comparative perspective. The seminars combine theoretical discussions led by leading international scholars with collaborative group work and opportunities for academic exchange, fostering dialogue across legal traditions, the development of comparative research methodologies, and the establishment of international scholarly networks. The 2025 edition focused on the right to take collective action and featured talks by Tonia Novitz (University of Bristol), Benjamin I. Sachs (Harvard University), Antonio Lo Faro (University of Catania), Julia López López (Universitat Pompeu Fabra), Andrea Iossa (Kristianstad University), and Elisabeth Brameshuber (University of Vienna).

As part of the seminar, I participated in collaborative group research examining the different forms of collective action and their legal regulation across jurisdictions. Working comparatively across the legal systems of Italy, France and the United Kingdom, our group explored the wide range of forms that collective action may take, before examining the legality and regulatory frameworks governing each form within the respective national contexts. The group presented its findings, highlighting both the diversity of collective action and the differences and commonalities in its legal treatment across the three jurisdictions.
